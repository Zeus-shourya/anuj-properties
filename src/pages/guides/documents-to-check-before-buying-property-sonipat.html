Documents to check before buying property in Sonipat

A practical checklist for a resale plot, house, floor or flat in a Sonipat township, with the official Haryana portals you can use to verify each item yourself.

Updated 13 October 2026By Anuj Properties, Sonipat

The checklist

  1. Ownership and title
  2. Loans, disputes and encumbrances
  3. The colony and the project
  4. Developer and maintenance dues
  5. Property ID and No Dues Certificate
  6. Houses, floors and flats
  7. The agreement and payments
  8. Registration and after

1. Ownership and title

  • The seller's title document: registered sale deed or conveyance deed. In a township, this may instead be the developer's allotment letter with every transfer endorsed since.
  • The chain: earlier deeds showing how the property passed to the seller. Every link should be registered.
  • Revenue record: the latest Jamabandi (Fard) and mutation entries for the land. You can check owner details on the Revenue Department's website.
  • Identity: the seller's Aadhaar and PAN match the names on the title. If the property has joint owners, all of them must sell and sign.

2. Loans, disputes and encumbrances

  • Encumbrance certificate: apply online on the Jamabandi portal to see registered charges against the property.
  • Court cases and stay orders: search the court case entries recorded against the land.
  • Home loan: if the seller has a loan on it, get the bank's letter on the outstanding amount and how the loan will be closed and the original papers released.
  • Declaration: the agreement should state that the property is free of loans, disputes and other claims.

3. The colony and the project

  • Licence: private colonies and townships need a licence from Haryana's Department of Town and Country Planning. Check on tcpharyana.gov.in, and use the Revenue Department's land status check.
  • RERA: projects that must register with Haryana RERA can be looked up in the HRERA project search. Parsvnath City, for example, appears there under registration no. 344 of 2017.
  • Layout plan: the plot number, size and road widths match the approved layout.
  • Section 7A NOC: some sales of vacant land in notified urban areas need a No Objection Certificate from the District Town Planner before registration. Ask whether it applies to the plot you're buying.

4. Developer and maintenance dues

  • A no-dues letter from the developer and from the maintenance agency or RWA.
  • What the developer needs to record a resale (transfer request, NOC, fee), and who pays the transfer charges.
  • That the developer's records show the seller as the current owner.

Parsvnath Developers Ltd has been in a corporate insolvency resolution process since 30 April 2026. If you're buying in Parsvnath City and the developer's no-dues or NOC is needed, get the current procedure in writing before you pay.

5. Property ID and No Dues Certificate

For property within municipal limits, a sale deed can't be registered without a No Dues Certificate (NDC) showing that property tax and other municipal dues are paid. The NDC is generated against the property's Property ID on the ULB Haryana property portal, and is valid for three months. Ask the seller to arrange it early, since corrections to a Property ID can take time.

6. Houses, floors and flats

  • Approved building plan, and construction that matches it.
  • Occupation certificate under the Haryana Building Code. For houses in licensed plotted colonies, empanelled architects can self-certify it.
  • For a builder floor: the floor, the land share, roof or terrace rights and parking written into the deed.
  • For a flat: the society's no-dues certificate, parking allotment and maintenance charges.
  • Utilities: paid-up electricity and water bills, and connections in the seller's name.

7. The agreement and payments

  • An agreement to sell with the price, payment schedule, possession date, what happens if either side backs out, and the seller's declarations.
  • Payments by bank transfer or cheque, with receipts. Avoid cash.
  • If the price is Rs 50 lakh or more, the buyer must deduct TDS from the payment to the seller. Ask your CA for the current rate and process.
  • Budget for stamp duty and registration charges, worked out on the higher of the price and the collector-rate value.

8. Registration and after

Haryana's paperless registration needs Aadhaar e-KYC for both buyer and seller, and the system checks that the seller is the owner in the revenue record. After registration, mutation starts automatically. Check a few weeks later that your name appears in the Jamabandi, and keep the originals safe. See documentation support for the full process.
