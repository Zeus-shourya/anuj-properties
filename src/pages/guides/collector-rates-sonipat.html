Collector rates in Sonipat (2026-27)

What a collector rate is, why it matters when you buy, sell or pay property tax, and where to find the official 2026-27 list for your area.

Updated 13 October 2026By Anuj Properties, Sonipat

What is a collector rate?

A collector rate (often called a circle rate elsewhere) is the minimum value per unit that the district administration sets for property in each village, sector or colony. It exists so that stamp duty is not charged on an under-declared price. The district Collector fixes the rates segment by segment, publishes drafts for objections, and then notifies the final list, under the Indian Stamp Act and Haryana's rules against undervaluation.

Sonipat's 2026-27 rates

The final collector rates for 2026-27 came into force on 1 April 2026. Reports at the time noted increases of up to 75% in parts of the district; agricultural land in one village, for example, went from about Rs 1.43 crore to Rs 2.5 crore per acre. Increases differ a lot from one segment to the next, so look up the exact entry for the property you're dealing in.

Where to check the official rates

  1. Sonipat district website: the collector rates page has the final 2026-27 lists, with separate files for Sonipat, Gohana, Rai, Kharkhoda, Khanpur and Ganaur.
  2. Haryana Revenue Department: the collector rate search.
  3. Jamabandi portal: collector rates on jamabandi.nic.in.

In the Sonipat lists, residential and commercial rates are given per square yard, agricultural land per acre, and industrial (HSIIDC) land per square metre. Find the tehsil, then the village, sector or colony, then the right category.

How collector rates affect you

  • Stamp duty: charged on the higher of the deed price and the collector-rate value. See stamp duty and registration charges.
  • Property tax: since 1 August 2026, urban property tax in Haryana is worked out on the basis of collector rates, so a higher rate can mean a higher tax bill.
  • Capital gains: if a property is sold below its collector-rate value, the tax department can take the higher value when working out the seller's gains. Ask your CA.

A simple illustration

Suppose a colony's collector rate for residential plots were Rs 20,000 per square yard. This is an example figure, not an actual rate. A 200 sq yd plot there would have a minimum value of 200 × Rs 20,000 = Rs 40 lakh.

  • Agreed price Rs 70 lakh: stamp duty is charged on Rs 70 lakh.
  • Agreed price Rs 35 lakh: stamp duty is still charged on Rs 40 lakh.

Collector rate vs what your property will sell for

The collector rate tells you the floor, not the market. Two plots in the same segment carry the same collector rate, yet a corner or park-facing plot in a well-occupied block can sell for considerably more than a middle plot in an emptier one. For a realistic market figure, ask for a price opinion.
