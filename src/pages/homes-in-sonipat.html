Independent houses (kothis)

A kothi gives you the land and the freedom to renovate or add a floor within the building rules. In a resale kothi, look at the age and quality of construction, damp and seepage, the water source, and whether any extra floors or extensions were built with an approved plan. Unapproved construction can cause trouble when you sell or take a loan.

Builder floors

Builder floors are separate floors in a low-rise building on one plot, each with its own sale deed. They're popular because they feel like a house but cost less. Before buying a floor, get these in writing:

  • which floor and how much of the land share comes with it,
  • who owns the roof or terrace, and any right to build above,
  • which parking space (stilt or front) is yours,
  • a separate electricity meter and the water arrangement,
  • how shared costs such as the lift, stairs and outside walls are split.

Flats and apartments

In a group housing society, check the maintenance charges and what they cover, power backup, parking allotment, the society's no-dues certificate for resale, and for newer projects the HRERA registration and the occupation certificate. Compare flats on carpet area, not super area.

3 BHK homes

We deal in 3 BHK houses and floors in both townships. When comparing them, look past the bedroom count: the carpet area, natural light, which floor it is, parking, and the direction it faces make a bigger difference to daily life and resale value.

Checks before you buy a resale home

  • Sale deed chain in the seller's name, and the seller's identity matches
  • Approved building plan, and completion or occupation certificate
  • Property tax paid, and electricity and water bills cleared
  • Maintenance, society or developer dues cleared, in writing
  • No loan or mortgage outstanding, or a bank's letter on how it will be closed
  • An inspection for seepage, cracks, wiring and plumbing
