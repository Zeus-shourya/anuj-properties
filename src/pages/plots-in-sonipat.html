Plots we deal in

Most of the plots we buy and sell are in the two townships we know best: Parsvnath City in Sector 8, where our office is, and Omaxe City. We also help with plots in other parts of Sonipat. Within any township, plots differ more than their size suggests:

  • Corner plots: open on two sides, with more frontage, light and air.
  • Park-facing plots: an open, green view in front.
  • Wide-road plots: easier access and parking than an inner lane.
  • Regular (middle) plots: usually the best value for the size.
  • East- or north-facing plots: often preferred by buyers who follow Vastu.

Tell us the size, budget, facing and the block you prefer, and we'll shortlist plots that are actually for sale, then walk the site with you.

How plot prices are worked out

Resale plots are normally quoted as a rate per square yard multiplied by the plot size. Two plots of the same size in the same township can still be priced quite differently, because the rate moves with:

  • the block and how developed and occupied it is,
  • corner or park-facing position, and road width,
  • facing, and the shape of the plot (a regular rectangle is easier to build on),
  • whether the developer's and maintenance dues are clear,
  • how many similar plots are for sale at the moment.

On top of the agreed price, budget for stamp duty and registration fees, any transfer charges the developer levies on resale, pending maintenance dues if the seller hasn't cleared them, and brokerage. Our guide to stamp duty and registration charges in Haryana explains the government costs.

We don't publish price lists because rates change with every deal. Ask us on WhatsApp for the current asking rates in the block you're interested in.

Before you pay a token: a plot checklist

  • Ownership: the seller's sale deed or conveyance deed, or the developer's allotment letter with every transfer since, in the seller's name.
  • Plot identity: plot number, block, size and dimensions match the approved layout plan and what's on the ground.
  • Dues: written confirmation that the developer's dues, maintenance charges and property tax are paid.
  • Transfer process: what the developer needs for a resale transfer (no-dues certificate, NOC, fee) and who pays it.
  • Encumbrances: no loan, mortgage or court case against the plot. Ask the seller to declare this in the agreement.
  • Everyone signs: if the plot has joint owners, all of them must sign the agreement and the sale deed.

The full list, with what each document is for, is in our guide to documents to check before buying property in Sonipat. For the paperwork itself, see documentation support.
